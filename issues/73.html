    Trump formally rejects Iran's seven-day Hormuz plan, calling it 'unacceptable' as officials eye strikes after the midterms›

    President Trump said Saturday he has rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, telling reporters the plan — a full ceasefire, an end to the US naval blockade, sanctions relief and roughly $12bn in unfrozen Iranian assets in exchange for reopening the strait and restarting nuclear talks — was "not acceptable." US officials cited by multiple outlets say Trump is privately skeptical Tehran would follow through and still expects to resume strikes after November's midterms, even as mediators describe this week's indirect talks as "positive and constructive." Iran said Saturday it is still awaiting an "official" US response and considers the diplomatic channel open.

    Why it mattersa formal, on-the-record rejection — not just a leaked preference — confirms the diplomatic track is stalled, not merely slow, while Hormuz transit stays near zero.
    What to doDon't price in any near-term Hormuz reopening — Trump's on-record rejection, paired with officials' expectation of post-midterm strikes, pushes any resolution past November at the earliest.
