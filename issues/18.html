    Iran calls Trump's "total control" claim a miscalculation as Hormuz traffic hits a fresh low and DP World's Dubai throughput collapses 86%›

    The narrative and the physical data keep diverging further, not converging. President Trump said this week the US now holds "total control" of the Strait of Hormuz behind a naval "wall of steel"; Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called that a miscalculation, and Tehran again says the strait stays blocked until its own conditions are met. The ships back Tehran's read, not Washington's: Kpler tracked just 8 vessel transits on Tuesday, below the 10-day average of about 12 and the lowest single-day count since Aug 5, with the five-day average still pinned near 13 — close to a three-month low and roughly 90% below the pre-war daily average of about 130 ships. War-risk insurance keeps pricing the same reality, with Gulf transit premiums holding at 3% to 10% of a vessel's hull value, up from about 0.25% before the war. The clearest confirmation now comes from a company's own books: DP World's Jebel Ali container throughput collapsed 86% in the second quarter, to 374,000 TEU from 2.7 million a year earlier, and profit fell 39% to $585 million — and rather than wait out a reopening, the operator is now building bypass terminals in Fujairah, on the Gulf of Oman side of the strait. Adding to the pressure, a fatal Aug 11 Houthi strike on the cargo ship Tihamah killed six in the adjoining Bab-el-Mandeb strait, the first shipping deaths in that campaign in over a year — and Yemen's government and the Houthis still can't agree on whether the ship was carrying food or weapons.
