    Brent slides below $100 as Mideast oil exports near pre-war levels, even as Trump rejects Iran's Hormuz reopening offer›

    Brent settled at $99.89 a barrel Tuesday, down 6.5% from Monday's $106.89, as Kpler-tracked Middle East crude exports climbed to roughly 16.3 million b/d in September — the highest since the war began in February, though still about 3.2 million below pre-war levels — after Saudi Arabia restarted its Hormuz-bypassing East-West pipeline. Trump on Sept 26 rejected Iran's seven-day proposal to reopen the strait in exchange for lifting the US naval blockade and Iranian oil sanctions, calling it unacceptable, while mediators kept talks running in New York. Independent trackers still disagree sharply on Hormuz's own share of that flow, from roughly 5 million to 13 million barrels a day.

    Why it mattersGenuine de-escalation — falling prices and recovering exports — is now competing with a formal US rejection of Iran's own reopening terms; the gap between market-signaled relief and diplomatic deadlock is the real story, not either side's framing alone.
    What to doWatch Kpler's own weekly export tally against Lloyd's List's next Hormuz Brief rather than either government's statements — the wide gap between trackers (5-13 million b/d) shows this data is still contested.
