    Hormuz shipping collapses to near-zero as the 60-day US-Iran deadline lapses unmet — DP World's own earnings confirm the damage›

    Kpler ship-tracking shows the Strait of Hormuz effectively shut over the weekend: five vessels transited Saturday, and Sunday's count fell to somewhere between zero and three depending on the tracker, against 31 the previous weekend — a 64% collapse from the post-ceasefire peak. The 60-day US-Iran memorandum, signed in Islamabad on June 18, expired Monday with neither side having implemented its terms; Iran has set six conditions for further talks, including lifting the US naval blockade and compensation for war damage. President Trump said Aug 14 he will "declare the Hormuz Strait a territory of the United States" once Iran is "defeated"; Tehran's deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi replied the strait "has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian." Physical reality backs the ship-tracking data rather than contradicting it: DP World's own H1 2026 results show Jebel Ali throughput down 59.5% year-on-year, with the second quarter alone down 90.1% to 374,000 TEU, an outage now costing the operator roughly $100 million a month. Brent crude closed at $88.31 a barrel on Aug 17.
