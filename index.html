    Iran and Oman finalize a Hormuz "shipping map," but Washington isn't party to it and Brent crude jumps 6% on the holdout›

    The Hormuz diplomatic track has moved from principle to detail. Iran and Oman have finalized a full "shipping map" as part of a broader bilateral accord — an arrangement both governments describe as independent and sovereignty-preserving, including a proposed joint coordination center to manage maritime traffic and collect vessel information, and the closure of temporary routes near Iran's Larak Island and through Omani territorial waters. Iran's state media has not spelled out whether ships will be charged transit fees or what security arrangements apply, with a further round of talks still to come. The catch is who isn't in the room: the United States was not party to the negotiation and is considered unlikely to accept terms that fall short of restoring free passage along the Persian Gulf-to-Arabian Sea route it says its Navy now controls after minesweeping — a claim Tehran continues to reject outright. The market is pricing the gap between progress and resolution: Brent crude has gained more than 6% this week, trading near $90 a barrel, as hopes fade for a deal that actually increases ship traffic rather than just formalizing a bilateral map. Vessel counts back that skepticism — just eight ships crossed the strait on Monday, against a pre-war daily average near 120-130 — while war-risk insurance keeps pricing the same reality, with Gulf transit premiums holding at 3% to 10% of a vessel's hull value, up from about 0.25% before the war. The clearest confirmation remains a company's own books: DP World's Jebel Ali container throughput collapsed 86% in the second quarter, to 374,000 TEU from 2.7 million in the first quarter, while group net profit fell 39% even as group-wide revenue rose 13.1% to $12.7 billion on the rest of its global portfolio — and rather than wait out a reopening, the operator is building bypass terminals in Fujairah, on the Gulf of Oman side of the strait, fully outside it.
