    Bessent's "Economic D-Day" finally lands as "Operation Economic Outcast" — but Tehran gets a grace period, and Hormuz stays nearly empty›

    Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's long-teased sanctions arrived Monday as "Operation Economic Outcast," unveiled from the Treasury's Cash Room and aimed, in his words, at severing "every economic lifeline" sustaining Iran's 92 million people "until Tehran stands alone." The package designates more than 60 individuals, entities and vessels, adds digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping as newly sanctionable categories, and singles out Iran's state bank by name — "every branch of Bank Melli must be shuttered and dark," Bessent said — alongside Shahr Bank and two Dubai exchange houses, Titan Exchange and Alps International, accused of laundering oil revenue for the National Iranian Oil Company and Naftiran Intertrade back to the IRGC-Qods Force through networks spanning the UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore and Switzerland. Yet the toughest enforcement isn't immediate: Bessent said he is "giving everyone the opportunity to remedy bad behavior" before penalties bite, and the administration has confirmed China — Iran's largest oil buyer — will not be exempt from the campaign despite that grace window. Oil fell anyway: Brent lost 2.5% to $92.06 a barrel and WTI slid the same to $85.65, later revised to $84.89, as traders booked profits from last week's rally rather than pricing any actual resolution. The physical picture underneath the sanctions news hasn't moved: Kpler counted just seven tankers transiting Hormuz on Aug 21 (four in, three out), commercial crossings are running at roughly 4-13 ships a day against a pre-crisis baseline near 130, and flow through the strait sits near 40% of its normal 15-million-barrel-a-day rate now that the 60-day US-Iran ceasefire window has lapsed without a deal. India's own markets caught the same nerves Monday: the Sensex closed down 171.72 points (-0.22%) at 77,369.11 and the Nifty fell 32.95 points (-0.14%) to 24,219.05, both citing Hormuz tension directly. Iran's Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei's Sunday warning that Tehran would let "not a single drop of oil" leave the Gulf if the "economic war" continues still stands unanswered by Monday's announcement.
