    Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to commercial shipping as war-risk insurance costs stay near record highs

    Vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz totaled 84 in the week of July 27 to August 2, up from 45 the prior week, though non-Iranian-linked traffic rose to only 52 transits from 28. Both figures remain far below the roughly 73-a-day pace considered normal before the conflict, and most crossings move under naval escort. War-risk insurance for tankers in the region has held at 3% to 10% of a vessel's hull value, up from about 0.25% before the conflict — meaning a $100 million tanker carries $3 million to $10 million in additional insurance cost for a single voyage, versus roughly $250,000 previously.

    Today's Read

    • The Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea are moving in opposite directions this week: Hormuz transit volume is recovering slightly and Iran and Oman have finalized proposed shipping-route coordinates, even as war-risk insurance remains 12 to 40 times higher than before the conflict and the Houthi movement says it will begin targeting Saudi oil tankers in the northern Red Sea.
    • US trade policy widened further: new forced-labor tariffs of 10% to 12.5% now apply to 60 trading partners including India, on top of existing country-specific rates, and separate Section 338 tariffs take effect August 19 covering nearly all imports. Carriers are still cutting capacity — about 8% of Asia-linked sailings through early September — even as container freight rates fall for a third straight week.
    • India added real capacity this week: Vizhinjam's automated terminal has final Customs clearance for full export-import operations from August 18, Adani Energy Solutions won a roughly $1 billion transmission contract for a green hydrogen hub in Andhra Pradesh, and L&T's green-ammonia supply deal with ITOCHU has moved from joint development to a signed long-term offtake agreement.
