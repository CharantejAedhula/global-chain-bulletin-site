    Iran hardens Hormuz reopening demands hours after the UAE confirms an Iranian missile struck an ADNOC tanker in the strait›

    Iran's Supreme National Security Council added new preconditions on August 9 — a full lift of the US naval blockade, a troop withdrawal and a permanent end to the war — before it will fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz, hardening its position even as Tehran and Oman finalize coordinates for a provisional safe-transit route first announced August 5. Hours earlier, the UAE confirmed an Iranian missile struck an ADNOC tanker transiting the strait, with no casualties reported; ADNOC says more than a dozen of its vessels have now been hit since February. President Trump said August 9 the US is "low-keying" talks and shifting toward economic pressure instead. Ship-tracking data still shows crossings swinging between roughly 4 and 17 vessels a day, a fraction of the 130 to 140 daily transits the strait carried before the war, and war-risk insurers continue charging 7.5% to 10% of a tanker's hull value per transit, up from about 0.25% before the war.
