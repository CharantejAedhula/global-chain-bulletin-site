    Iran offers seven-day plan to reopen Hormuz as Trump reportedly rejects it, eyeing decisive action after the midterms›

    Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told reporters in New York on Thursday that Tehran has offered US mediators a seven-day plan: a full ceasefire including Lebanon, an end to the US naval blockade, sanctions waivers and release of roughly $12bn in frozen assets, in exchange for reopening Hormuz within seven days and restarting nuclear talks. US media reported Friday that Trump has told aides he is not rushing to accept it and expects a return to strikes after November's midterms, even as mediators call the talks "positive but early."

    Why it mattersa detailed reopening offer sitting next to a reported plan to resume bombing after the midterms confirms neither side is actually near a deal.
    What to doDon't reprice Hormuz risk lower on the offer alone — a seven-day roadmap with concrete conditions is more substantive than August's collapsed 14-point MOU, but Trump's own reported timeline runs past the midterms, not seven days.
