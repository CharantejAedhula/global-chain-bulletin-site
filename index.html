    Hormuz transit inches up from zero but stays crushed, as Iran and Oman near a shipping-route deal and Trump renews his threat to bomb Oman›

    Kpler-tracked traffic through the Strait of Hormuz ticked up to six commodity vessels on Monday — three exiting the Gulf, three entering, with no large crude or LNG tankers among them — against a trailing 10-day average of just 11 ships a day and a pre-crisis baseline several multiples higher; roughly 520 commercial vessels remain backed up in the Arabian Gulf, with clearance estimated to take up to eight weeks even if transit fully resumes today. The 60-day US-Iran memorandum formally expired Monday with Trump declaring he isn't in a rush to extend it. The diplomatic action has shifted to a separate, US-excluded channel: Iran and Oman are reported close to finalizing a new Hormuz shipping-route arrangement routing traffic partly through Iranian waters with a joint maritime coordination center, though Iran's foreign ministry stresses this alone would not reopen the strait, which remains conditioned on separate US concessions. Trump repeated — for a second time — his threat to "bomb the shit out of" Oman if it "gets in the way" of a deal; Oman's government has not responded publicly. Brent crude settled at $91.02 a barrel Tuesday, its third straight rise.
