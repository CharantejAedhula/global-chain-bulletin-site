    Iran's Rezaei threatens to halt every drop of Gulf oil as Bessent's “Economic D-Day” sanctions near their 2pm ET unveiling›

    Iran's escalation deepened today: Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei warned that Tehran would let "not a single drop of oil" leave the Strait of Hormuz or anywhere else in the Persian Gulf if the United States' "economic war" continues, and cast any country that joins Washington's campaign as committing an "act of war." It follows Sunday's Financial Times op-ed in which Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wrote that "at dawn begins an economic D-Day — the single greatest financial offensive ever marshalled against an adversary," with the sanctions package — aimed at Iran's oil exports, its banks, and the foreign firms and governments still trading with it — still due for a 2pm ET unveiling, not yet delivered as of this issue. Iran's parliamentary National Security and Foreign Policy Commission has separately backed a draft law making ships passing through Hormuz pay for "services" Tehran claims to provide, while the state-run Persian Gulf Strait Authority has warned that vessels violating its transit rules could face fines, seizure or confiscation. None of it has yet reversed the physical trend Kpler identified last week: more than 80% of liquids transits over the past two weeks have used the UN-authorized Omani lane or gone dark to obscure their path, up from almost none a month ago, and Lloyd's List Intelligence's own count still shows just 73 total transits in the week of Aug 10-16, down from 91 the week before, with no fresher weekly rollup published since. Brent crude fell 1.38% to $93.09 a barrel on Monday — WTI slid 1.62% to $85.65 — as traders booked profits ahead of the sanctions rollout rather than any actual de-escalation, unwinding part of last week's 5%-plus rally. India's own markets caught the same nerves: the Sensex closed down 171.72 points (-0.22%) at 77,369.11 and the Nifty fell 32.95 points (-0.14%) to 24,219.05, both citing the Hormuz tension directly. The 60-day ceasefire window both sides were meant to hit has now lapsed without a deal, closing off the formal truce mechanism aimed at ending the six-month-old Middle East war.
