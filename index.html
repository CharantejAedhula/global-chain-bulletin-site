    Iran effectively loses the Strait of Hormuz as tanker traffic collapses and Iranian missiles land in UAE waters›

    Independent vessel tracking puts Hormuz transits at roughly six inbound tankers Tuesday — thirteen total crossings including outbound, plus two dark tankers — against a pre-crisis baseline of about 73 ships a day, with the waterway now widely described as effectively closed to commercial shipping. Iran's foreign ministry says reopening conditions don't exist "as long as the U.S. naval blockade continues," and the 60-day US-Iran memorandum lapsed Aug 17 with no extension or fresh talks announced. The escalation sharpened further on Aug 18, when the UAE Defense Ministry said it detected two ballistic missiles fired from Iran, one landing in Emirati territorial waters — triggering the country's first nationwide emergency alert since Jul 12. US forces have redirected 64 vessels, disabled three and boarded two as of Aug 17, per United Against Nuclear Iran's tracker, while roughly 520 commercial vessels remain backed up in the Gulf. War-risk premiums have climbed to 7.5-10% of hull value, up from 1-3% weeks earlier and a pre-crisis 0.25% — roughly $21 million on a single $210 million VLCC transit. Brent crude settled near $91.86 a barrel Wednesday, its fourth consecutive daily rise.
