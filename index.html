    Hormuz crossings fall again to just 8 vessels on August 7 as Tehran calls Oman talks "positive" — the strait stays near-closed regardless

    The Strait of Hormuz saw only 8 confirmed crossings on August 7, down 33% from the day before, with most vessels using Iran's unilateral safe-passage scheme, according to tracking firm Kpler — continuing a week that totaled just 33 transits Monday through Thursday versus 50 in the prior week. Iran's government has called its talks with Oman on reopening the strait "advancing positively," and President Trump has renewed demands for a deal within days, warning of "total surrender" if talks collapse again. But the physical count keeps telling a flatter story than the diplomacy: the strait has been effectively closed since the US and Israel opened a war on Iran on February 28, and Tehran is reported to be rebuilding military strength during the current lull rather than standing down. War-risk insurers are still quoting $10 million to $14 million to cover a single large tanker's transit, roughly forty times pre-war rates, while Bab-el-Mandeb crossings 800 miles south moved the opposite direction, rising 18% to 26 vessels the same day.
