    Lead · Impact Score 10

    Hormuz traffic collapses to a single tracked transit on Aug 16 as the UAE's total break with Iran deepens and Brent extends its run to $93.86›

    IMF-run PortWatch's vessel-tracking data recorded just 1 transit through the Strait of Hormuz on Aug 16 alone, against a pre-crisis baseline of roughly 73 ships a day — the starkest single-day reading yet of how thin traffic has become within the week Lloyd's List Intelligence separately clocked at 73 total transits (Aug 10-16), down from 91 the week before. The tanker boarded near Qeshm Island on Aug 17-18 is now assessed by trackers as seized rather than merely detained, and is reported holding stationary off the Khasab approach in the North Hormuz corridor; Iranian officials say the vessel was held for "violating Iranian maritime laws" and non-payment of transit fees, a justification the UAE and international trackers reject. The crisis deepened sharply on Aug 19, when the UAE's Foreign Ministry announced it is suspending all trade, commercial and financial dealings with Iran "until further notice," a day after detecting two ballistic missiles fired toward its territory, one of which fell inside UAE waters — the culmination of a run of strikes on ADNOC's own tankers that saw two hit Aug 13-14 and a third shortly after, bringing the total to 18 ADNOC vessels struck since the war began. The roughly $28 billion trade relationship had supplied more than 30% of Iran's imports (about $21 billion) and taken in some $7 billion, or 13%, of its exports; the embargo lands on an economy the IMF's July update forecasts will contract 5.4% this year amid 68.9% inflation. Tehran, for its part, says the strait will not reopen until the US "corrects" its behavior — language that closes off any near-term diplomatic off-ramp. US-Iran diplomacy has "effectively collapsed" per Lloyd's List, with the 60-day memorandum having lapsed Aug 17 with no extension. War-risk premiums remain at 7.5-10% of hull value, up from a pre-crisis 0.25% — roughly $21 million on a single $210 million VLCC transit. Brent crude has now risen for a sixth consecutive session, reaching $93.86 a barrel Friday, as cargo increasingly reroutes through Fujairah, Khor Fakkan, Oman and the Jeddah land bridge.
